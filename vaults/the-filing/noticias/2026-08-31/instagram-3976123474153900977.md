## Descrição

Dolly Parton was heading home to Brentwood, Tenn., when she turned on the radio and almost crashed her Cadillac. The words coming out of the car speakers were hers, written some two decades before, but she was hearing them delivered by Whitney Houston for the first time. “I had to pull off to the side of the road, because it just got bigger and bigger and better and better,” she wrote in “Dolly Parton: Songteller.”⁠
⁠
You know the track. Parton wrote a lot of songs, but none pump through the American bloodstream like “I Will Always Love You.” She managed to have a country hit with three different versions of it, while Houston’s mountain-moving 1992 rendition became one of the bestselling singles of all time. The track is rife with contradictions: A breakup ballad—effectively saying “it’s not you, it’s me”—disguised as a pledge of devotion. An emotionally overwrought song used savvily as a coldblooded bargaining chip. An expression of eternal dedication to a man who later sued Parton for $3 million.⁠
⁠
That was Porter Wagoner, a country star with a taste for rhinestone-flecked Nudie suits. His eponymous television show, which featured performances from him and others, was viewed by several million people a week at its peak. He hired Parton in 1967 to replace another singer, Norma Jean Beasler, even though he had reservations about her voice. “It was real skinny, real wiry, real kind of irritating actually; listening to it almost hurt your head,” Wagoner said in Stephen Miller’s “Smart Blonde: The Life of Dolly Parton.” ⁠
