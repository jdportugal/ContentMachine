## Descrição

When Salim Ramji became CEO two years ago, Vanguard Group had long been one of the investing world’s biggest players, with $9 trillion of customer assets. Today, Vanguard has jumped to $13 trillion. Its flagship exchange-traded fund, Vanguard S&P 500 (known by its ticker VOO), recently became the first ETF to hit $1 trillion in assets. And in June, it temporarily surpassed BlackRock’s iShares as the largest ETF provider in the U.S. Now the two firms are in a dead heat, each with some $4.7 trillion in ETF assets.⁠
⁠
Ramji, who joined from BlackRock, is quick to acknowledge that the foundations of Vanguard’s success were laid down by his predecessors, especially Jack Bogle, who founded the company in 1975. Bogle was a tireless advocate for low-cost, passive investing in index funds. That strategy triumphed, and so did Vanguard. Ramji’s challenge is very different from Bogle’s, but no less formidable: Achieve still further growth while keeping costs low and continuing to put investors first.⁠
⁠
For Ramji, the answer is to apply the company’s investor-friendly mission and relentless focus on cutting costs for customers to three main areas ripe for growth: high-yield cash savings, financial advice, and fixed-income investing.⁠
⁠
Dominating these competitive businesses—and bringing the so-called Vanguard effect to these areas—won’t come easy. It will require taking market share from powerful competitors like Charles Schwab, Fidelity, and BlackRock. And it will require spending heavily on technology and people—tricky for a company that prides itself on keeping costs low for customers.⁠
