## Descrição

Inside an Omaha test kitchen, executives at Conagra, the maker of Slim Jims and Hungry-Man dinners, recently huddled over plastic trays of microwaved eggs, potatoes and sausage. One by one, they lifted a package of cheese sauce and poured out the liquid in a slow orange ribbon.⁠
⁠
Ashley Lind, who heads behavior science at Conagra, studied the breakfast bowl in front of her. She considered: Was it satisfying?⁠
⁠
No one had tasted the food yet. That was part of the point: Conagra is trying to design products that will entice even people who don’t want to eat.⁠
⁠
The tasting was one of dozens of tests Conagra is conducting with an eye toward the conundrum presented by GLP-1 weight-loss drugs. The medications’ power to erase feelings of hunger threatens to upend the industry’s model for growth, where success has often meant selling as much food as possible.⁠
⁠
“They just lose the craving, the desire for food,” said Bob Nolan, Conagra’s senior vice president of growth science. Since the drugs broke into the mainstream in 2023, Nolan and his team have been trying to decipher what food might still appeal to GLP-1 users, and why.⁠
⁠
As many as 55 million Americans, or 15% of the population, are expected to be on GLP-1 drugs by 2035, according to projections from Morgan Stanley. KPMG estimates users consume about one-fifth fewer calories while on the medications.⁠
⁠
With one in five U.S. households now including a GLP-1 user, doubled from the start of 2025 according to PwC, the drugs are increasingly hard to ignore.⁠
⁠
“It’s one of the most profound changes we’ll ever see,” said Barb Stuckey, an executive at food research and development firm Mattson, whose clients have included major food companies like Conagra.⁠
