## Descrição

As high-school math students gear up to buy the newest version of Texas Instruments’ TI-84 graphing calculator, cheaper upstarts are trying to break the company’s decades-old grip on the market.⁠
⁠
Texas Instruments first launched the TI-84 in 2004. Now the company is out with a new version, the Evo, which debuted in April with faster processing speeds and a home screen that features icons for different tools.⁠
⁠
Graphing calculators are no longer the novelty they were roughly half a century ago, and the cost of components like LCD screens and processors has fallen since then. Still, Texas Instruments has been able to command premium prices for its calculators, largely because many high-schoolers have no choice but to buy them. The Evo has a $159.99 list price, though Target is currently selling it for $85.99 and other retailers are offering deals as well.⁠
⁠
Some say customers aren’t exactly paying for innovation, but rather for decades of familiarity on the educator side — along with textbooks that build the calculators into their curricula.⁠
⁠
“Students and parents don’t choose calculators,” University of Arizona law and business professor Barak Orbach told MarketWatch. “It’s the school that dictates for them.”⁠
⁠
As 39 states move to restrict cellphones in classrooms, the Evo is doubling down on what made the TI-84 a staple in classrooms, functioning as a distraction-free device that fulfills academic math needs.⁠
⁠
The company owes its success in part to a smart strategic choice that has helped it take over the U.S. classroom. In the 1980s, while Sharp, HP and Casio marketed their scientific calculators to professionals, Texas Instruments was focused on penetrating the education space. In 2022, TI reported that it had sold more than 90 million graphing calculators since 1990.⁠
⁠
Texas Instruments teamed up with math educators and textbook publishers to create an ecosystem for teacher training programs and instructional materials that would seamlessly standardize TI across American classrooms.⁠
