## Descrição

Americans are abandoning the belief that smarts and effort are the best ways to get ahead in life, according to new research, underscoring the pessimism many people have about their chances at financial success.⁠
⁠
The gloom has a lot to do with high prices and wages that are struggling to keep up.⁠
⁠
Fewer than four in 10 Americans said they believe talent and hard work matter most for financial success, according to a six-year survey of more than 28,000 people by the FrameWorks Institute, a nonprofit research organization focused on how people view the economy, government and society.⁠
⁠
In stark contrast, 65% think the top factor for people and groups that get ahead is that they have better opportunities.⁠
⁠
That’s a big change from just a few years ago. As late as mid-2022, more people said hard work mattered more in paving a path to financial success.⁠
⁠
“We are seeing a sea change,” according to Andrew Volmert, senior vice president of programs at FrameWorks. Waning belief in past beliefs about getting ahead is seen across a range of demographics, including political affiliation and income level, he said.⁠
⁠
In June, a record-low 35% of surveyed people believed hard work and talent mattered most for financial success, he noted.⁠
⁠
Americans continue to value hard work, Volmert said. “They’re just less convinced that hard work is enough, given how the economy works.”⁠
⁠
FrameWorks researchers have been measuring Americans’ moods on economic mobility since 2020. ⁠
⁠
The belief in the “meritocratic mindset” really started to fall off in the October 2022–to–March 2023 time period, according to polling. There’s now a roughly 30-percentage-point difference between the share of people who believe that success is linked to hard work versus the idea that success comes from having better opportunities.⁠
⁠
The timing isn’t coincidental, Volmert said. “We believe that the rise in inflation decreased people’s faith, and our society’s ability to make sure that everyone gets a fair shake.” ⁠
