## This computer is technically alive

It does not just simulate a brain. Part of it was grown from one.

---

## Grown, not manufactured

Alive is the accurate word: Cortical Labs, now operational in Singapore, cultivates human neurons from stem cells directly onto a chip. The hardware is part silicon, part living tissue.

---

## 200,000 neurons per unit

On that chip, roughly 200,000 human neurons receive and return electrical signals. They are not passive components; they adapt and respond to what flows through them.

---

## It learns by being alive

That adaptation is the point: neurons rewire themselves in response to stimulation. Where silicon must be retrained, living tissue reorganizes on its own.

---

## Thirty watts. That is all

And it does this on almost nothing: the entire biological server rack draws about 30 watts, a fraction of what conventional AI hardware burns. A light bulb's worth of power, doing computation.

---

## Why efficiency changes everything

That gap matters because energy is becoming AI's hardest limit. Data centers strain power grids; a brain runs on the energy of a sandwich. Biology solved efficiency millions of years ago.

---

## Still a prototype, not a product

To be clear about where this stands: it is early, and its real capabilities remain to be proven. But the path it opens, toward complex problem solving on living substrates, is now physically real.

---

## The machine is becoming biology

Which leaves the real question: for seventy years we built computers to imitate thought. Now we are wiring thought itself into computers. Follow Brand Machine to track where the two converge.
