not grounding with visual primitives, it

I guess that makes a lot of sense. But,
I think the most impressive part is that
this is just not a simple toy experiment

Then, after ViT, they apply 3 by 3
spatial token compression, which means
every nine neighboring patch tokens get
merged into one visual token. And then,
once those tokens enter the LLM, CSA
further compresses every four visual
tokens into one KV cache entry. So, for
a 756 by 756 image, the raw image has
571,000 pixels, right? So, the ViT first
turned this into 2,916 patch tokens, and
after the 3 by 3 compression, this
becomes 324 visual tokens. And after CSA
compression, this becomes only 81 visual
KV cache entries. So, from raw pixels to
final KV cache, this gives around 7,056
times compression ratio, which is kind
of wild. While the comparison isn't
apples to apples at the level of visual
token compression per 14 by 14 patch,
DeepSeek's pipeline uses 36 times fewer
KV cache entries than the plain Lava
style projection setup. This setup kind
of goes against the perception gap that
previous multimodal models are trying to
solve, but maybe the bottleneck isn't
just how many pixels you see. It's
whether or not your visual reasoning can
actually reason well enough. So, one
possible reason they took the paper down

covered by the predicted trajectory.
These two are important because

Like if you only check predicted points,

on-policy distillation to close the gap

specialists. So after all these cool
