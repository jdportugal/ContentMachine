## Descrição

CERN is ditching IBM-owned Red Hat rather than replacing thousands of computers that still work.

The research center behind the Large Hadron Collider, the world's largest and most powerful particle accelerator, is moving more than 2,200 control computers toward Debian Linux after newer versions of Red Hat Enterprise Linux stopped supporting many of the older CPUs CERN still relies on.

Red Hat has been owned by IBM since IBM bought the company for roughly $34 billion in 2019. 

For CERN, the hardware change became a huge problem. 

RHEL 9 would leave about 47% of its embedded control computers unsupported. 

RHEL 10 raises the requirements again, affecting another 17% of the remaining machines. 

These are not normal office PCs. 

Many sit underground and connect directly to CERN’s accelerators and custom hardware. Replacing them could mean redesigning 11 custom boards, rewiring parts of the system and spending at least CHF 5.4 million.
 
So CERN chose another option: leave IBM-owned Red Hat behind and move the systems to Debian, which can keep much of the existing hardware running.

IBM bought Red Hat. Red Hat changed its hardware requirements. CERN decided switching Linux was better than replacing thousands of working computers.
