## Descrição

Scott Galloway's warns about what happens to the US economy if AI stops being expensive.

The NYU Stern professor calls it "AI dumping." His view is that China is deliberately pushing cheap and free open-weight models into the US market to destroy the pricing power of American AI labs, the same play he says worked in solar panels and steel.

The mechanism is pricing. If enterprises can get most of the capability from a Chinese open-weight model for a fraction of the cost, the multi-million dollar site licenses stop getting signed, growth at OpenAI and Anthropic slows, and the valuations built on that growth stop making sense.

That is where it stops being a tech story. Galloway puts roughly 40% of the S&P 500 as tied to the AI bet, so a repricing of AI is a repricing of the index. He also says most US GDP growth over the past 2 years came from AI capital spending, a figure economists dispute. The largest cloud companies are on track to spend roughly $670 billion in 2026, more than 90% of their expected cash flow.

His scenario does not require AI to fail. It requires AI to get cheap enough that nobody can charge much for it.
