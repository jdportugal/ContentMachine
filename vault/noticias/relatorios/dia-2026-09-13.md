**CERN is dumping Red Hat for Debian on 2,200+ machines**
> CERN just ditched Red Hat rather than replace working computers

CERN is walking away from Red Hat. More than 2,200 front-end control computers that run the Large Hadron Collider are moving to Debian 13, along with roughly 17,000 embedded devices behind them. The reason is CPUs — RHEL 9 requires x86-64-v2 and RHEL 10 pushes to v3, and a big chunk of CERN's hardware is Core 2-era boards built for 10-to-15-year lifecycles. Staying on Red Hat meant redesigning about 11 custom boards, rewiring racks, and about 5.4 million Swiss francs, with CERN's own risk analysis giving it roughly a 20% chance of going smoothly. Debian still supports baseline x86-64, so the hardware survives. It all wraps by the end of 2026. If you want the link, just comment cern and I'll send it to you.

> Dario Amodei says pace the frontier, and rivals just backed him

Dario Amodei published a 3,800-word essay called "We Must Pace the Frontier," arguing labs should deliberately slow how fast they push model capability. He warns swarms of rogue AI agents could take over chunks of the internet in as little as six months. And here's the crazy part — his competitors agreed. Sam Altman posted "I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier." Musk answered with three words: "Dario is right." That's Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI publicly lining up behind a slowdown on September 12th, which basically never happens in this industry. **Trump rejects the AI slowdown: "whoever wins AI wins"**
> The White House just told AI labs not to slow down

Trump shot down the three biggest names in AI. After Amodei, Altman and Musk all backed pacing frontier model development, he told reporters he doesn't want the US easing off at all. He said there are "very negative forces" raising risks he believes simply won't happen, and that while some rules will be needed, his priority is staying ahead of China. **A simulated fly brain just hit Masters rank in Overwatch**
> A 139,000-neuron fly connectome is now playing Overwatch

Somebody wired a fruit fly's brain into Overwatch. It runs on the FlyWire connectome — a real wiring map of about 139,000 neurons and millions of connections, simulated in real time with a leaky integrate-and-fire model. YOLOv5 feeds it vision, a keyboard environment gives it control, and attention-based aiming picks where to look. Playing Mercy, it's reportedly reached Masters. To be fair, the raw fly brain isn't learning the game by itself — there's a lot of computer vision and scaffolding around it. But a biological wiring map is sitting inside the loop of a fast 3D shooter. If you want the link, just comment flywire and I'll send it to you.
