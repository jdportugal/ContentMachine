## Síntese

**Moonshot AI releases Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weights model**
Moonshot AI put the weights for Kimi K3 up for free download on 26 July 2026, making it the largest open-weight model ever released — 2.8 trillion parameters in a sparse mixture-of-experts design (896 experts, 16 activated per prompt) with a 1-million-token context window and two new architectural pieces, Kimi Delta Attention and Attention Residuals. It took the top spot on the Frontend Code Arena index in blind preference testing, beating leading US proprietary systems including Claude Fable 5, and it carries roughly 75% more parameters than the previous largest open model, DeepSeek V4. This matters because the working assumption that open weights trail closed labs by three to six months no longer holds at the frontier — though the practical catch is a ~1.4 TB download and API pricing of $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output.

---

**AI companies are buying antique books in bulk, scanning them, and shredding the originals**
A 404 Media investigation published 21 July 2026 identified ISBNdb as a broker marketing physical-book acquisition to AI developers at volumes of 1,000 to 1 million titles per order, with buyer anonymity and NDAs included. Pre-2022 print commands a premium precisely because it predates generative text — it is guaranteed to be human-written and free of AI slop. The destruction is a cost choice rather than a technical necessity: machines like the Treventus ScanRobot digitize fragile volumes non-destructively at 2,500 pages per hour, but cutting spines off is cheaper at industrial scale, and a 2025 US court ruling treated destructive scanning of legally purchased books as fair use. One caveat worth keeping: neither ISBNdb's marketing nor the public reporting ties a specific completed order to a named AI buyer.

---

**A humanoid robot collapsed on stage during Qualcomm's Computex 2026 keynote**
The failure happened in Taipei during the unveiling of the Dragonwing IQ10 Robotics Reference Design, a 700 TOPS platform Qualcomm is positioning as a production-ready brain for humanoids, autonomous mobile robots and industrial systems. The machine was a 4NE-1 from NEURA Robotics, an early-access partner, and it folded moments after the presenter told the audience the technology was already here; staff covered it with a tarp and carried it off while the talk continued. Qualcomm later described the incident as a controlled shutdown triggered by a communication glitch, with the robot executing a designed safe-collapse sequence — an explanation that, true or not, underlines how far the sim-to-real gap still is from being closed in embodied AI.

---

**A Yahoo job listing asked for 10+ years of experience with Claude Code**
The posting, for a Senior Software Developer role, specified a decade of experience with a tool that only entered public preview in February 2025 — barely a year and a half in the wild. Boris Cherny, the Anthropic engineer who built Claude Code, would fall roughly nine years short of qualifying. It's a small thing, but it's a symptom of a real hiring problem: recruitment templates are demanding seniority in tools that are younger than the requisition process itself, which filters out exactly the people who actually know them.

---

**Morgan Stanley built AlphaLab, an autonomous multi-agent system for quant research**
Developed by the bank's ~30-person AI research group, AlphaLab takes only a dataset and a natural-language objective, then runs the full experimental cycle unsupervised: it explores the data and writes a research report, builds and adversarially validates its own evaluation framework, and runs large-scale GPU experiments through a Strategist/Worker loop that accumulates findings in a persistent playbook. Tested with GPT-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.6 across three domains, it produced CUDA kernels averaging 4.4× faster than torch.compile (up to 91× on individual cases), 22% lower validation loss on LLM pretraining versus a single-shot baseline, and 23–25% gains over standard traffic-forecasting baselines. What makes it notable is that all domain-specific behavior is generated by the model itself as adapters, so one unmodified pipeline transfers across qualitatively different problems — the pitch being that an algorithm built for credit bonds can be re-derived for munis without human porting.
